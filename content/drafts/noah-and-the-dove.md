God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the cattle that were with him in the ark. God had shut them in, and for five months the ark had drifted on the flood. Now God stopped the fountains of the deep and shut the windows of heaven, and held back the rain. He sent a wind over the water, and the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat. The water went back from the earth continually, until the tops of the mountains showed. Still Noah waited, forty days more.

He opened the window he had made. There was water on every side, and nothing green anywhere.

He sent out a raven first. It flew out over the water and back, out and back, until the flood was gone from the earth. Noah took a dove and let her go from the window, to see if the water had gone down.

She found no rest for the sole of her foot, because the water still lay on the face of the whole earth. Noah saw her coming back to him, low over the water. He put out his hand and took her, and pulled her in to him, into the ark. Her cold feet closed around his finger.

He waited yet another seven days. Noah opened the window again, and he held the same dove in his two hands a long time before he sent her out over the water.

The dove came back to him in the evening, and in her mouth was an olive leaf, freshly plucked. So Noah knew that the water had gone down.

He waited yet another seven days. This time, when he held the dove at the window and opened his hands, she did not come back to him any more.

Noah was six hundred and one years old when the new year came. On the first day of that year he climbed up and took the covering off the top of the ark, and looked.

Behold, the face of the ground was dry.
