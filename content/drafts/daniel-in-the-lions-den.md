The men who ruled Babylon under King Darius could not bear it. The king meant to set Daniel over the whole kingdom, because an excellent spirit was in him. They searched for a fault in him and found none. "We will never catch this Daniel," they said, "unless it has to do with the law of his God."

The men came crowding in to the king. "King Darius, live for ever!" they said, and they asked him for a new law. No one in the kingdom might ask anything of any god or any man for thirty days, except of the king. Whoever did would be thrown into the den of lions. "Sign it, O king," they said, "like the law of the Medes and Persians, which altereth not." The king signed it.

Daniel knew that the writing was signed. He went into his house all the same. His room was under the roof, and its windows were open toward Jerusalem. There he knelt three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks to his God, as he had always done.

His enemies came running together and stood in the street below those windows. From there they could see him on his knees. They went straight back to the king.

"Did you not sign a law," they asked, "that anyone who asks anything of any god or man for thirty days, except of you, shall be thrown to the lions?"

The king answered, "The thing is true, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which altereth not."

They answered, "That Daniel, the captive from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or to the law you signed. He prays to his God three times a day."

When Darius heard these words, he was sore displeased with himself. He set his heart on delivering Daniel, and he labored till the going down of the sun to find a way out of his own law. The men were already waiting for him. "Know, O king," they said, "that by the law of the Medes and Persians, nothing the king has signed may ever be changed."

The king commanded it, and they brought Daniel and cast him into the den of lions. Before the stone was laid on its mouth, Darius spoke to him. "Your God, whom you serve continually, he will deliver you." The king sealed the stone with his own ring, and his lords sealed it with theirs, so that nothing could be changed for Daniel. The king went back to his palace and sent away his supper and his musicians, and his sleep went from him.

He rose very early and went in haste to the den. He cried out with a lamentable voice. "O Daniel, servant of the living God, is your God, whom you serve continually, able to deliver you from the lions?"

From under the stone a voice answered. "O king, live for ever! My God has sent his angel and has shut the lions' mouths, and they have not hurt me. My God found me innocent, and before you, O king, I have done no hurt."

The king was exceedingly glad for him. He commanded that Daniel be taken up out of the den, and they lifted him out. No manner of hurt was found on him, because he believed in his God.
