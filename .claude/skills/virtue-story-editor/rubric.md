# Rubric

Score each dimension 1 to 5 with the quoted line that earns it. 5 is
magazine-ready with nothing to change; 4 is strong with one small fix; 3 is
competent and forgettable; 2 shows a drift pattern; 1 breaks a `writing.md` rule.

| # | Dimension | 5 looks like | 2 looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Opening** | The actor acts in the first two sentences; the listener feels what could go wrong by the end of paragraph one; setup is folded into the scene. | Scenery, chronicle, or a premise before anyone does anything. |
| 2 | **Telling detail** | A detail per scene that only this story owns and a reader would repeat. | Stock detail: pounding hearts, cool water, soft light. |
| 3 | **The turn** | The beat where knowledge or action changes is shown by an event and not given away early. | Announced, missing, or spent in advance ("one last time"). |
| 4 | **Virtue order** | The virtue is visible in an act before any narrator label; at most one label. | Labels in paragraph one or several in a row. |
| 5 | **Read-aloud breath** | Every long sentence puts its verb early and its stress word last; pauses fall where the source turns; refrains and chants survive. | Front-loaded clauses, monotony, a source chant cut for length. |
| 6 | **Diction and restraint** | Concrete nouns and active verbs; feeling carried by gesture; nothing explained twice. | Stacked adjectives, narrator explaining a gesture. |
| 7 | **Ending** | The source's strongest final beat at full strength (action, speech, consequence, or image); the child gets the payoff; nothing after it. | A quiet image of the writer's own in place of the source's payoff; "From that day"; a second moral. |
| 8 | **Depth** | The source's complication survives: a cost, an irony, an open edge an adult keeps thinking about. | Sanded into a greeting card. |
| 9 | **Fidelity** | Every event and quoted line traces to the governing text; its best phrases are kept, not paraphrased flat. | Invented speech for a real person, a legend asserted as fact, a source line flattened. |
| 10 | **Freshness** | House voice, not formula: no sentence frame, ending gesture, pet construction, invented refrain, or paragraph build a blind reader can quote from another house story. | Quotable formula: the same "and"-chain restoration close, So/Then openers, a planted echo, a repeated frame. |
| 11 | **Field craft** | Dek makes a parent choose it; reflection is 20 to 60 true, warm words; alt and prompt match the scene. | SEO dek, homily reflection, generic alt. |

## Atlantic bar

Yes or no with evidence; every answer must be yes for `publish`.

1. Could the first paragraph belong only to this story?
2. Is there a detail a reader will retell?
3. Does every paragraph change something: what a character knows, where they
   stand, or what is at stake?
4. Is there nothing left to cut? (Name the weakest sentence and defend it.)
5. Is the ending the strongest beat the source offers, at full strength, with
   nothing after it?

## Hunt list

- A stated emotion beside the gesture that already showed it.
- A sentence opening on a participle or a stack of clauses.
- Rhetorical symmetry: "not X but Y," paired abstractions, triads.
- A detail that is pretty but does no work.
- Dialogue that explains the plot to the listener.
- Modern idiom in an old world ("process," "focus," "okay," "deal with").
- A word a six-year-old would not know that context does not explain.
- A source refrain, chant, or famous phrase that was cut or flattened.
- A closing sentence that would survive being moved to `virtueDescription`,
  unless the governing text itself closes on it ("because he believed in his
  God"); then keep it, and make sure `virtueDescription` does not repeat it.

## Verdict thresholds

- `publish`: zero lint errors; the draft wins or ties both blind comparisons in
  step 3a (governing text, and bedtime rival when the brief names one) on
  read-aloud, scene, and ending; the step 3b style read, when run, cites
  fewer than two quotable shared tics (blind-judge.md; a bare "one hand" verdict
  does not block); every dimension 4 or 5; mean 4.4 or
  higher; every Atlantic-bar answer yes. A `publish` is final only when Margaret
  adds her `Sign-off`.
- `kill`: the source cannot be verified or is a modern copyrighted retelling; the
  story has no decisive act a child can see; or three or more dimensions score 2
  or lower after one full revision.
- `revise-with-notes`: everything else, every note a line edit marked `must` or
  `should`.
