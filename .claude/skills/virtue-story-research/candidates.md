# Starter candidates by canonical virtue

Public-domain sources that are not yet in the catalog as of 2026-10-13. Already
published, so excluded: Androcles and the Lion, David and Mephibosheth, The Cherry
Tree, The Woodcutter's Axe, The Three Men (the fiery furnace), The Boy and the
Filberts, The Ant and the Grasshopper, Dietrich Bonhoeffer. Drafted, so taken:
Noah and the Dove, The Bell of Atri, Daniel in the Lions' Den. Run
`hub-counts.mjs` (`SKILL.md` step 1) before you commit to one; this list goes
stale. No candidate here appears as an example in `craft.md` or `fields.md`; keep
it that way, so no candidate arrives half-written.

Each line: title, oldest source, the **decisive act**, and the risk to manage.
"Angle" names a sub-virtue that is not yet in `SUB_VIRTUE_TO_CANON_SLUG`; using it
as the `virtue` label needs an engineering line first.

## Prudence

- **Alexander and Bucephalus.** Plutarch, *Life of Alexander* 6. The boy notices
  the horse is afraid of its own shadow and turns its head into the sun. Prudence
  as attention. Risk: the father's boast at the end reads as a moral; let the
  horse carry it.
- **The Judgment of Solomon.** 1 Kings 3:16-28. The king calls for a sword to find
  the true mother. Risk: the threat to the baby needs a calm, quick telling; it can
  also be read as Justice, so state the mapping reason.
- **The Sword of Damocles.** Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 5.61-62. A flatterer
  gets the king's couch, then sees the sword hung by a single hair. Risk: the
  choice is the king's lesson, not the guest's; keep it a scene, not a parable
  explained.
- **The Blind Men and the Elephant.** John Godfrey Saxe's poem (1872), after the
  Buddhist *Udana*. Risk: an idea story with no single chooser; works best short.
- **The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse.** Aesop (Jacobs, 1894). The country mouse
  chooses safety over a feast. Risk: thin; may run under 350 words with a reason.
- **Joseph and the Seven Years of Grain.** Genesis 41. Storing grain against
  famine. Risk: overlaps The Ant and the Grasshopper; pick only if the hub needs
  scripture.

## Justice

- **Aristides and the Shell.** Plutarch, *Life of Aristides* 7. A voter who cannot
  write asks Aristides to scratch "Aristides" on the shard that will exile him;
  he does. Risk: the word "Just" is in the source; it must stay the farmer's
  complaint, not the narrator's praise.
- **The Bell of Atri** (drafted). Longfellow, *Tales of a Wayside Inn* (1872), also Baldwin
  (1896). A starving old horse rings the town's bell of justice, and its master is
  made to keep it. Justice owed to the old and useless.
- **Zacchaeus.** Luke 19:1-10. A tax collector in a sycamore tree promises to repay
  fourfold. Strong image; angle: restitution.
- **Fabricius and Pyrrhus.** Plutarch, *Life of Pyrrhus* 20-21. A Roman envoy
  refuses gold, then warns his enemy of a poisoner. Risk: two scenes; choose one.
- **The Emperor's New Clothes.** Andersen (1837); use a 19th-century translation,
  not Hersholt. Honesty maps to Justice already. Risk: long; keep the child's one
  line as the turn.
- **The Boy Who Cried Wolf.** Aesop. Honesty, told from the cost. Risk: the wolf
  eats the sheep; keep it offstage.
- **Damon and Pythias.** Cicero, *De Officiis* 3.45; Valerius Maximus 4.7. A man
  returns to die so his friend's pledge is honored. Angle: fidelity. Risk: an
  execution frame; the tyrant's pardon must arrive cleanly.

## Fortitude

- **Grace Darling.** The *Forfarshire* wreck off the Longstone light, 7 September
  1838; Baldwin (1896). A keeper's daughter rows into the storm with her father.
  Risk: real history; verify every number against contemporary accounts.
- **David and Goliath.** 1 Samuel 17. A shepherd refuses Saul's armor and takes five
  stones. Risk: the beheading; end at the stone.
- **Horatius at the Bridge.** Livy 2.10; Macaulay's *Lays of Ancient Rome* (1842).
  Three men hold a bridge while it is cut down. Risk: battle violence; keep it to
  the bridge and the river.
- **Harriet Tubman.** Sarah Bradford, *Scenes in the Life of Harriet Tubman*
  (1869). Choose one documented crossing. Risk: real history; no invented dialogue.
- **Bruce and the Spider.** A Scottish legend first printed by Sir Walter Scott
  (1828); Baldwin (1896). Angle: perseverance. Could map to Hope instead; decide in
  the brief.
- **The Hero of Haarlem.** Mary Mapes Dodge, *Hans Brinker* (1865), chapter 18.
  A boy holds back the sea through the night. Risk: an American invention; the
  source note must say so.

## Temperance

- **The King and His Hawk.** Baldwin, *Fifty Famous Stories Retold* (1896), a
  legend of Genghis Khan. In anger the king strikes down the hawk that kept spilling
  his cup, then finds the poisoned spring. Angle: self-control. Risk: the source's
  closing moral line belongs in `virtueDescription`.
- **King Canute on the Seashore.** Henry of Huntingdon, *Historia Anglorum* (12th
  century); Baldwin (1896). The king sets his chair before the tide to silence his
  flatterers. Angle: humility.
- **Diogenes and Alexander.** Plutarch, *Life of Alexander* 14. Asked what he wants,
  the philosopher asks the king to step out of his sunlight. Angle: contentment.
- **The Golden Touch.** Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 11; Hawthorne, *A Wonder-Book* (1851).
  Risk: the daughter turned to gold is Hawthorne's; name which version you tell.
- **Esau and the Pottage.** Genesis 25:29-34. A hungry man trades his birthright for
  a bowl of red stew. Short and sharp.
- **Cincinnatus.** Livy 3.26-29. Called from his plough to rule Rome, he gives the
  power back in sixteen days. Can also map to Justice.
- **The Fisherman and His Wife.** Grimm (1812), Lucy Crane translation (1882).
  Greed in rising wishes. Risk: long and repetitive; the repetition is the
  read-aloud pleasure, so ask for a length exception.

## Faith

- **Daniel in the Lions' Den** (drafted). Daniel 6. Knowing the decree is signed, Daniel kneels at his window toward Jerusalem and prays, as he always has. The act is that he changes nothing; stop before 6:24.
- **The Juggler of Our Lady.** The 13th-century *Del Tumbeor Nostre Dame*; Anatole
  France, "Le Jongleur de Notre-Dame" (1892). A tumbler with nothing else to give
  performs his tricks before the altar. Angle: devotion.
- **The Widow of Zarephath.** 1 Kings 17:8-16. A widow bakes her last handful of
  meal for a stranger, and the jar does not empty. Charity angle is possible.
- **Ruth and Naomi.** Ruth 1. "Whither thou goest, I will go." Angle: fidelity.
  Risk: little action; the choice is spoken on a road.
- **Peter Walks on the Water.** Matthew 14:22-33. He steps out, looks at the wind,
  and begins to sink. The turn is built in.

## Hope

- **Pandora's Jar.** Hesiod, *Works and Days* 42-105; Hawthorne, "The Paradise of
  Children," *A Wonder-Book* (1851). After the troubles fly out, something small is
  still knocking inside. Risk: Hope's escape is Hawthorne's; say so in `source`.
- **Elijah Under the Juniper Tree.** 1 Kings 19:4-8. A prophet who asked to die
  wakes to bread baked on hot stones and a jar of water by his head. Strong
  telling detail.
- **Hagar in the Wilderness.** Genesis 21:14-19. A mother sets her son under a bush
  and sits a bowshot away; then she sees the well. Risk: the near-death of a child;
  tell it gently and quickly.
- **The Ugly Duckling.** Andersen (1843), a 19th-century translation. Endurance
  through one hard winter into spring. Risk: long; needs an exception or a strict
  cut to the winter and the spring.
- **Bruce and the Spider.** See Fortitude; Hope is the better mapping if the
  despair, not the effort, is the center.

## Charity

- **Saint Martin's Cloak.** Sulpicius Severus, *Life of Saint Martin* 3 (about
  397). A soldier cuts his cloak in half for a freezing beggar at the gate of
  Amiens. One sword stroke; a perfect image.
- **Sir Philip Sidney and the Cup of Water.** Fulke Greville, *Life of Sidney*
  (1652); Baldwin (1896). Wounded, he passes his water to a dying soldier: "Thy
  necessity is yet greater than mine." Risk: thin; under 350 words needs a reason.
- **The Good Samaritan.** Luke 10:25-37. Keep the lawyer's question as the frame or
  cut it; decide in the brief.
- **The Widow's Mite.** Mark 12:41-44. Two small coins against the rich men's gifts.
  Risk: very short source; add no invented backstory.
- **King Alfred and the Beggar.** A legend of Alfred and Saint Cuthbert; Baldwin
  (1896). The hiding king shares his last loaf.
- **Where Love Is, God Is.** Tolstoy (1885), a pre-1929 English translation. A
  cobbler waits for a visitor and helps three strangers instead. Risk: long; ask
  for an exception or cut to the three visits.
- **The Lion and the Mouse.** Aesop. Overlaps Androcles; use only for a very young
  reader set.
